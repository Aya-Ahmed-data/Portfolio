Excel · Power Query · IBM Maximo · DP World

Key Insights
  • 23–30 days (8 WOs): These are urgent — must be closed immediately to maintain compliance before they exceed the 30-day threshold.
  • 31–90 days (6 WOs): Already past the compliance window — work orders that cross 30 days without closure are likely to keep aging. Immediate action required.
  • 91–180 days (8 WOs — highest bucket): All PM type. Once a WO exceeds one month, the data shows it tends to remain open for much longer. These need escalation.
  • 365+ days (1 WO): ⚠ Critical backlog — a single PM order left unresolved for over a year.
Key Conclusion
  • The pattern is clear: work orders that pass the 30-day mark are very likely to keep aging — the bulk of open WOs sit in the 91–180 day range. Fast action on the 23–30 day bucket is critical to prevent them from becoming long-term backlog.
Recommendation
  • Treat all WOs in the 23–30 day bucket as top priority for immediate closure to stay compliant.
  • Escalate the 91+ day WOs to department supervisors (CRN · WSP · UBI) for urgent follow-up.
  • Investigate root causes behind PM aging — why are preventive orders taking longer than corrective ones?
Business Impact
  • Reduced report generation time by approximately ⚡ 65% through full automation.
  • Eliminated manual classification of work orders by aging category.
  • Enabled department-level visibility through automatic sheet splitting per team.
  • Gave maintenance supervisors a consistent, up-to-date view of backlog status.

Fuel Consumption Per Hour Analysis

Excel · Power Query · Jan–Jun 2026 · DP World

Developed a structured fuel consumption reporting workflow to analyze equipment fuel usage and monitor consumption trends per operating hour across RTG, MHC, and MTH equipment types. Automated data processing and reporting to reduce repetitive manual work and provide a consistent view of equipment fuel performance over a 6-month period (Jan–Jun 2026).

Avg. Fuel per Hour by Equipment Type (L/hr) — Jan–Jun 2026

RTG — Monthly Fuel Consumption (Liters) · Anomaly Detected

Key Insights
  • MTH equipment recorded the highest average fuel consumption rate (~53 L/hr), nearly 2.6× more than RTG units (~20 L/hr), indicating heavier operational load.
  • March showed a notable spike across MHC and MTH types — MHC106 peaked at 93.9 L/hr and MTH001 at 138.9 L/hr — flagged as abnormally high and worth further investigation.
  • January recorded the highest total consumption (424K liters), with a consistent decline through March, followed by stabilization around 300K liters from April to June.
  • Top consumers in June were MHC units (MHC012, MHC110, MHC011), each exceeding 19,000 liters for the month.
Recommendation
  • Investigate March spike in MHC and MTH units — abnormal L/hr rates may indicate mechanical inefficiency or data entry issues.
  • Set monthly L/hr benchmarks per equipment type to enable automated flagging of consumption outliers.
Business Impact
  • Streamlined recurring fuel consumption reporting, reducing reliance on manual data compilation.
  • Improved visibility of equipment fuel efficiency through standardized per-hour metrics.
  • Enabled early identification of abnormal consumption patterns to support proactive maintenance decisions.
